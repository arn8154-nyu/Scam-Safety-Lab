These text messages are made up, so nothing you tap here can hurt you. Decide if each one is safe or suspicious, and see what to look for.

Why practice?

Scam texts pretend to be from a bank, a delivery company, or even a family member, to trick you into sending money or personal information.

They are very common. In 2024, people in the U.S. reported losing $470 million to scams that started with a text message. Fake package delivery texts were the most reported kind.

Practicing here, with made-up messages, helps you spot the warning signs and check a message safely before a real one reaches your phone.

Source: Federal Trade Commission (FTC), 2025.

6 practice messages, about 10 minutes

How does this work?
  1. Read a text message, just like one on your phone.
  2. Choose Looks safe or Seems suspicious.
  3. See the signs that give it away, and what to do if you get one like it.

There is no timer, and nothing you choose is saved or shared.
